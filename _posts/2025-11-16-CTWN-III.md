Be $\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime) \equiv \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime, \mathcal{S})$ the cylindrical $\sigma$-algebra on $\mathcal{S}^\prime$, generated by the topological dual of $\mathcal{S}^\prime$, which is $\mathcal{S}$. $\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime)$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra making all evaluation functionals $(\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime), \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))$-measurable.

A **generalized stochastic process** is an $(\mathcal{S}^\prime, \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime))$-valued random element

$$ W : (\Omega, \mathcal{A}, P) \rightarrow (\mathcal{S}^\prime, \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime)) \;\;\; . $$

$W$ is not parameterized by an index set $T$ like a standard stochastic process but by a function space, $\mathcal{S}$ in our case. Broken down by individual state :

$$ W = {(W_\phi)}_{\phi \in \mathcal{S}} \;\; \text{ with } W_\phi(\omega) \equiv \langle W(\omega) \mid \phi \rangle \text{ for a } \phi \in \mathcal{S} \text{ pointwise. } $$

Since $\mathcal{S}^\prime = \mathcal{S}^\prime(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}) \otimes \mathbb{R}^d = {\mathcal{S}^\prime(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R})}^d$, we can of course think of $W$ as vector ${(W_1, \dots, W_d)}^\top$ of $(\mathcal{S}^\prime(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}), \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}))$-valued random elements.

Note that the law $P_W \equiv W_*P$ is a full probability measure on $\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime)$, but usually won't simply extend to a full probability measure on the Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}^\prime, \sigma(\mathcal{S}^\prime, \mathcal{S})) \supseteq \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime)$. In many cases, $P_W$ isn't even countable additive on $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}^\prime, \sigma(\mathcal{S}^\prime, \mathcal{S}))$ and therefore not even a full measure.

Now we define a **continuous-time white noise process** with mean $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and positive semidefinite covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ to be a random element $W \in \mathcal{L}^0(\Omega, \mathcal{A}, P; \mathcal{S}^\prime, \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime))$ , fullfilling the following two conditions:

Expectation :

$$ W_\phi \in L^1(\Omega, \mathcal{A}, P; \mathbb{R}) \; \wedge \; \mathbb{E}[W_\phi] = \mu^\top {\langle 1 \mid \phi \rangle} \;\;\;\; \forall \phi \in \mathcal{S} $$

Covariance structure :

$$ W_\phi \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{A}, P; \mathbb{R}) \;\;\;\;\wedge $$

$$ \mathrm{Cov}(W_\phi, W_\chi) = {\langle \, \phi \, , \, \Sigma \: \chi \, \rangle}_{L^2} \;\;\;\; \forall \, \phi, \chi \in \mathcal{S} $$

( $$ {\langle \, , \, \rangle}_{L^2} $$ is the $L^2$ inner product

$$ {\langle \, \phi \, , \, \chi \, \rangle}_{L^2} \equiv \int_\mathbb{R} {\phi(t)}^\top \chi(t) \, dt $$

from $\mathcal{S} \subset L^2 \subset \mathcal{S}^\prime$. Note that the term $\langle 1 \mid \phi \rangle$ above is not the $L^2$ inner product here ($1$ is not even an element of $L^2$) but the distribution $1 \in \mathcal{S}^\prime$, evaluated at $\phi \in \mathcal{S}$, which is simply the integral of the test function $\phi$. )
