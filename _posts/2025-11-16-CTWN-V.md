There is a canonical Gaussian cylindrical measure $\gamma_{L^2}$ on $L^2$, whose finite marginals $\gamma_F$ are 
$$\mathcal{N}(\mu, {(I_{L^2} \otimes \Sigma)}_{\mid F})$$, $F$ running through the finite-dimensional subspaces of $L^2$. 
$\gamma_{L^2}$ is specified by its characteristic functional on $i(\mathcal{S}) \subset L^2$ which combined with the 
inclusion gives the already known characteristic functional on $\mathcal{S}$ :

$$
  \Phi(\phi) = \exp \left(i \: \mu^\top {\langle \: 1 \: , \: \phi \: \rangle} - \frac{1}{2} \, {\langle \: \phi \: , \: \Sigma \: \phi \: \rangle}_{L^2} \, \right) \;\;\; .
$$

Therefore, by the Bochner-Minlos theorem, $\gamma_{L^2}$ extends uniquely to a Radon Gaussian measure on $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}^\prime)$, which coincides with the (extended) white noise law $W_*P$. 

We can't visualize a generalized stochastic process, like continuous-time white noise, with a plot right away. But we can plot them with appropriate filters applied. 
Here is our continuous-time white noise process $W$, filtered by linear low-pass (smoothing) filters aka Gaussian kernels of different width : 

$$
  W_\epsilon(t) = W * K_\epsilon(t) 
$$

( $K_\epsilon$ is a Gaussian kernel of width $\epsilon$ . )
