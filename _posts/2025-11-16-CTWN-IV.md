$ m(\phi) \equiv \mathbb{E}[W_\phi] \in \mathbb{R}$ is the mean functional of $W$. Its characteristic functional $\Phi_W$ is defined by

$$
  \Phi_W(\phi) = \int_{\mathcal{S}^\prime} e^{\: i \: \langle \: T \: \mid \: \phi \: \rangle} \,\, P_W(dT) = \mathbb{E}[e^{\,i \, W_\phi}] \;\; .
$$

Since the characteristic function of a $W_\phi$ is 

$$
   \Phi_{W_\phi}(t) = \mathcal{F}[P_{W_\phi}] = \mathbb{E}[e^{\,i \, t \, W_\phi}] \;\; ,
$$

we can recover $\phi_W$ by evaluating $\Phi_{W_\phi}(t)$ at every $\phi \in \mathcal{S}$ :

$$
   \Phi_W(\phi) = \Phi_{W_\phi}(1)
$$
﻿

Usually random fields, like continuous-time white noise, are constructed by creating a probability measure from a characteristic functional via the Bochner-Minlos theorem.

Be $W_\phi$ is a Gaussian random variable with 

$$
   W_\phi \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu^\top {\langle \: 1 \: , \:\phi \:  \rangle} \, , \, {\langle \: \phi \: , \: \Sigma \: \phi \: \rangle}_{L^2}) 
$$

and characteristic function 

$$
    \Phi_{W_\phi}(t) = \mathcal{F}[P_{W_\phi}] = \mathbb{E}[e^{\:i \: t \: W_\phi}] = \exp \left( i \: t \: \mu^\top {\langle \: 1 \:, \: \phi \: \rangle} - \frac{1}{2} \: t^2 \: {\langle \:  \phi \: , \: \Sigma \: \phi \: \rangle}_{L^2} \right)  \;\;\; .
$$

The characteristic functional of $P_W$ can now be recovered by evaluating it at every $\phi \in \mathcal{S}$  i.e.

$$
    \Phi_W(\phi) = \mathcal{F}[P_W](\phi) = \int_{\mathcal{S}^\prime} e^{\: i \: \langle \: T \: \mid \: \phi \: \rangle} \,\, P_W(dT) = \mathbb{E}[e^{\: i \: W_\phi}] \] \[ = \Phi_{W_\phi}(1) = \exp \left(i \: \mu^\top {\langle \:1 \:, \:\phi \: \rangle} - \frac{1}{2} \, {\langle \: \phi \: , \: \Sigma \: \phi \: \rangle}_{L^2} \, \right)\;\;\; . 
$$     

Since $\mathcal{S}$ is nuclear, the Bochner-Milnos theorem implies that $P_W$ extends uniquely to a Radon Gaussian measure on $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}^\prime) \supset \mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime)$.  ( $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{S}^\prime)$ w.r.t. the weak-*-topology on $\mathcal{S}^\prime$ ).
