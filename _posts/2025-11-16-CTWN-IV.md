The covariance property implies that $W$ is a generalized Gaussian process or equivalently that \(W_*P\) is not only a probability measure but even a Gaussian measure on the cylindrical $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S}^\prime)$. 

The $W_\phi$ s covariance fully determines their dependency structure. The inner product $${\langle\,\phi\,,\,\chi\,\rangle}_{L^2}$$ acts as a weight for how much $$W_\phi$$ and $$W_\chi$$ are correlated: If $\phi$ and $\chi$ have disjoint supports, then $${\langle\,\phi\,,\,\chi\,\rangle}_{L^2} = 0$$, making them uncorrelated. If $\phi$ and $\chi$ overlap significantly, then their inner product is nonzero, meaning $$W_\phi$$ and $W_\chi$ are correlated. 

Continuous-time white noise is not uncorrelated for all distinct test functions $\phi, \chi \in \mathcal{S}$. Instead, its covariance depends on the inner product ${\langle \, \phi \,,\, \chi \, \rangle}_{L^2}$ which is nonzero unless $\phi$ and $\chi$ are orthogonal. 

Uncorrelatedness arises naturally for functions with disjoint supports, but not necessarily for all distinct functions.

Note that continuous-time white noise is modelled as a generalized Gaussian process while discrete-time white noise need not be Gaussian. Discrete-time white noise is by definition identically distributed, continuous-time white noise is not.
